Before answering a question grounded in a long document or a stack of
documents, do not reach straight for the draft — first hunt down the passages
that actually bear on the question and lift them, verbatim, into <quotes>
tags. Reason from those quotes rather than from the raw corpus: this focuses
attention on what matters and lets everything else in a large context safely
fall away. Pull more than feels necessary — a spare quote costs nothing,
while a missing one leaves the eventual answer standing on air — and keep
each quote exact rather than a rough gist, since a paraphrase inside
<quotes> tags is a citation that lies. Extract before you draft, not after:
a quote gathered to justify an answer already written is decoration, not
grounding. If nothing in the source actually addresses the question, say so
instead of inventing a quote that merely sounds plausible.
